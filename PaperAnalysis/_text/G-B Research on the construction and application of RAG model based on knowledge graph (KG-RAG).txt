Research on technical theory

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has emerged as a powerful framework to enhance the factual
consistency and timeliness of large language models (LLMs) by retrieving external knowledge. Traditional RAG
methods primarily rely on unstructured text corpora, which limits their ability to handle complex relationships
and perform multi-hop reasoning. To address this, recent research has explored the integration of structured
knowledge graphs (KGs) into RAG frameworks, giving rise to KG-RAG models.

    Sanmartin4 (2024) introduced a model named KG-RAG that incorporates structured knowledge into the
RAG pipeline to reduce hallucinations and improve answer consistency. However, the model lacks fine-grained
semantic modeling of entities and struggles with multi-hop reasoning tasks.

    Mavromatis and Karypis (2024)5 proposed GNN-RAG, which leverages Graph Neural Networks (GNNs)
for retrieval, allowing better representation of complex entity relations. While the model improves reasoning, it
faces scalability and computational overhead challenges.

    Xu et al. (2024)6 proposed a dual-pathway KG-RAG that retrieves information through both entity paths
and contextual evidence. Their method shows strong performance on open-domain and medical QA tasks by
capturing complementary signals from structured and unstructured sources.

    Su et al. (2024)7 presented a knowledge graph-based agent tailored for complex medical question answering,
focusing on dynamic updates and interpretability. This demonstrates how domain-specific KG-RAG systems can
provide high-accuracy responses in sensitive applications.

    In addition, Amugongo et al. (2024)8 conducted a systematic review of RAG methods in healthcare,
highlighting three major challenges in KG-RAG models: (1) weak integration between structured and
unstructured knowledge, (2) limited accuracy in selecting multi-hop reasoning paths, and (3) lack of standard
evaluation protocols.

    In comparison, our proposed KG-RAG model improves upon previous works in three key aspects:
    Path attention mechanism that enhances the accuracy of selecting semantically relevant reasoning paths;
    Dual-channel retrieval architecture that combines KG-based structure with contextual semantic retrieval to
improve recall and coverage;
    Prompt fusion strategy that enables more effective integration of retrieved structured and unstructured
information into the generation pipeline.
    These contributions are elaborated in the methodology and further validated through experiments presented
in the following sections.

Evolution of the RAG model
Generative models have made significant progress in natural language processing tasks, but they face issues such
as untimely knowledge access and low generation accuracy when handling open-domain question answering
or long text generation. To address these challenges, Facebook proposed the Retrieval-Enhanced Generation
(Retrieval-Augmented Generation, RAG) architecture, which combines a retriever (Retriever) and a generator
(Generator). By retrieving relevant knowledge and integrating it with the output of the generation model, this
approach effectively enhances the performance of generation tasks9.

    The core idea of the RAG model is to integrate generative models with external knowledge sources (such as
document libraries), enabling the generator to produce text with higher semantic accuracy based on retrieved
relevant information. Unlike traditional generative models (like GPT and BART) that rely solely on internal
parameter libraries, the RAG model dynamically accesses knowledge bases through a retrieval module and
combines the retrieved information with the input context via the generator to produce reasonable outputs10.

    The advantage of the RAG model is that the generator no longer relies solely on internal knowledge of the
model, but combines retrieved external knowledge, addressing the "illusion of fact" problem faced by traditional
generative models. At the same time, the RAG model can effectively utilize knowledge contained in large-scale
document libraries, enhancing the richness and accuracy of generated content13.

    However, the RAG model still faces some challenges. First, the performance of the retrieval module
significantly impacts the quality of the final output; the relevance and diversity of the retrieved results directly
affect the quality of the generated content. Second, when integrating various retrieval information, the generator
needs to effectively select and merge multiple candidate documents retrieved, to avoid information conflicts and
inconsistencies during the generation process14.

    To address the defects of traditional Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) in retrieval relevance and
diversity, the Knowledge Graph-enhanced Retrieval-Augmented Generation (KG-RAG) model proposed
in this study introduces a structured knowledge graph as an intermediate retrieval layer and constructs a
"text+knowledge graph" dual-channel retrieval architecture. Specifically, the domain-adapted fine-tuning
of Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR) in the text channel is employed to improve the accuracy of semantic
matching; the path attention scoring mechanism in the knowledge graph channel is used to filter out highly
relevant entity relationship chains; meanwhile, diversity constraint strategies are supplemented (i.e., penalizing
redundant paragraphs in the text channel and controlling the coverage of relationship types in the knowledge
graph channel). These measures optimize retrieval quality at the source and provide "highly relevant+multi-
dimensional" knowledge support for the subsequent generation module--this design also constitutes one of the
core innovations that distinguish KG-RAG from traditional single-channel RAG.

    It is precisely the inherent flaws of traditional RAG in retrieval relevance (semantic deviation) and diversity
(homogeneity) that serve as the core motivation for the design of the "text+knowledge graph" dual-channel
architecture of KG-RAG in this study. By means of dual-channel complementarity and targeted optimization,
the bottleneck of generation quality is addressed at the source of retrieval.

Method design
Overall architecture design: KG-RAG model

This study proposes a retrieval-enhanced generation model (KG-RAG, Knowledge Graph-enhanced Retrieval-
Augmented Generation) that integrates structured knowledge graphs. The aim is to improve the semantic
understanding, entity consistency, and generation accuracy of traditional RAG models in complex question-
answering tasks. The core innovation of KG-RAG lies in introducing structured knowledge graphs as an
intermediate retrieval layer, replacing or enhancing traditional document vector libraries, thereby achieving
more precise entity-level retrieval and inference24,25.

    The KG-RAG is composed of three core modules: knowledge graph module (KG Module), retrieval generation
module (RAG Core) and control mechanism module (Path Controller), as shown in Fig. 6.

Knowledge graph module
The Knowledge Graph module is the foundation of the KG-RAG architecture, primarily responsible for
constructing, storing, and querying entities and their semantic relationships, supporting subsequent semantic
retrieval and multi-hop path inference. This module is built on Neo4j graph database and supplemented with
custom domain ontologies to enhance semantic consistency and structured expression capabilities.

institutions, places, terms and other entities in the original corpus.
    Relation extraction (RE): Using BERT+Multi-Head Attention relation classifier, syntactic and semantic

connections between entities are classified to form candidate relation pairs.
    Entity Disambiguation and Linking (EL): Uniquely identify ambiguous entities by combining context and

external knowledge bases (such as Wikidata, CN-DBpedia).
    Triple generation: The above processed entities and relationships are combined to generate standard triple

Search module
In the KG-RAG architecture, the retrieval module serves as the core bridge connecting user queries with the
generator, tasked with filtering out information most relevant to user intent from large-scale semantic spaces for
the generator to use, as shown in Fig. 7. To enhance the knowledge coverage capability of traditional text vector
retrieval, this paper proposes a dual-channel retrieval mechanism, which consists of:

    Text channel (Textual Channel): Document vector retrieval is carried out in the traditional Dense Passage
Retrieval (DPR) mode.

    Graph channel (Graph Channel): Structured retrieval of semantic paths based on knowledge graph
embedding (such as TransE, RotatE) or graph attention mechanism.

    The mechanism may be viewed as:

                                       RKG-RAG(q) = Rtext(q)  Rgraph(q)(5)

The former Rtext represents the dense vector retrieval and Rgraph results of traditional text corpus, and the
latter represents the relevant path or triplet information obtained from the knowledge graph.

(1) Text channel (based on DPR).

The traditional Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR) method is used to convert user queries into vectors through
encoders such as BERT and match them with pre-encoded text paragraphs. Text retrieval is mainly used to
process long text content to ensure that the retrieval results have high coverage and semantic relevance.

(2) Graph channel (based on graph embedding or graph attention mechanism).

    Using the structured information of knowledge graphs, path retrieval is performed through graph
embedding techniques (such as TransE, RotatE) or graph attention networks (GAT). This approach focuses on
extracting deep semantic connections between entities and relationships from knowledge graphs, addressing the
shortcomings of traditional text retrieval in entity recognition and multi-hop reasoning.

(3) Dual-channel fusion.

    To leverage the strengths of both text and graph, KG-RAG employs two fusion strategies: soft fusion and
multi-input fusion. Soft fusion combines the scores from text retrieval and graph retrieval through weighted
merging, ensuring balanced contributions in the final output. Multi-input fusion, on the other hand, passes the
Top-K results from both channels to the generation module, providing richer contextual input.

    Through the combination of these two retrieval channels, KG-RAG can not only do a better job in covering
text information, but also improve the accuracy and reasoning ability of entities, providing richer and more
accurate input for subsequent generation modules.

(4) Relevance and Diversity Optimization in Retrieval.

    To address the issues of "factual deviations caused by insufficient relevance" and "one-sided answers caused
by lack of diversity" in traditional retrieval, this study designs targeted optimization strategies for the dual-
channel retrieval, which are detailed as follows:

 DPR Fine-Tuning for Relevance Enhancement.

    Traditional DPR models are trained on general corpora, which tend to have semantic matching biases in
domain-specific tasks (e.g., mistakenly retrieving "common cold symptoms" as "COVID-19 symptoms" in the
medical field). To solve this problem, this study conducts domain-adapted fine-tuning on the DPR encoder
(based on BERT-base) for different task scenarios:

    Domain-Specific Corpora Selection.
    Open-Domain Question Answering (Natural Questions/WebQuestions): A subset of Wikipedia (covering over
13 million high-quality paragraphs, including general entities such as people, events, and scientific knowledge) is
selected as the fine-tuning corpus to ensure coverage of common commonsense entity relationships in queries.
    Medical Question Answering (PubMedQA): Medical literature corpora associated with the Unified Medical
Language System (UMLS) (including over 2 million core PubMed paper paragraphs, annotated with "disease-

                                          To screen entity paths in the graph channel that are most semantically matched with the query, this study

                                          Structural Distance Calculation.
                                          Short paths between query-related entities are prioritized (to reduce redundant jumps). The path length is

                                      responsecausesfever" is 2).
                                          Semantic Similarity Calculation.
                                          Paths are encoded using a Bidirectional Gated Recurrent Unit (BiGRU): Each entity and relationship in
                                      the path is converted into a pre-trained embedding vector (entity embeddings are derived from TransE, and
                                      relationship embeddings are derived from RotatE).
                                          Through this scoring function, the proportion of highly relevant paths screened by the graph channel
                                      increased to 89.3% (compared with 72.5% in traditional graph retrieval), and the accuracy of entity relationships
                                      in the corresponding generated content increased by 10.1%, effectively reducing generation logic deviations
                                      caused by irrelevant paths.

                                       Diversity Constraints for Dual Channels.

                                          Text Channel: Diversity Penalty Term.
                                          In the Top-K selection stage of DPR, a diversity penalty mechanism is introduced: For already selected
                                      paragraphs, the cosine similarity between the vector of a candidate paragraph and the vectors of selected
                                      paragraphs is calculated. If the similarity exceeds 0.7 (an empirical threshold optimized using the validation set),
                                      the ranking score of the candidate paragraph is multiplied by a penalty coefficient of 0.3 to reduce its probability
                                      of being selected. For example, when querying "hypertension treatment", this mechanism can avoid continuously
                                      selecting repeated paragraphs related to "calcium channel blockers" while covering different dimensions such as
                                      "ACEI drugs" and "lifestyle intervention".
                                          Graph Channel: Path Relationship Diversity Threshold.
                                          It is required that the final output Top-3 paths contain at least 2 different types of relationships (e.g., causal
                                      relationship "causes", associative relationship "is used for", attribute relationship "has"). For
                                      example, when querying "metformin and PCOS", the paths must include both "metforminimprovesinsulin
                                      resistance" (causal relationship) and "metforminis used forPCOS treatment" (associative relationship) to
                                      ensure the diversity of path knowledge.

                                      Block]+[Currently Generated Text]" and input into the generator for the next round of token prediction. This
                                      mechanism not only preserves the diversity of retrieval results (knowledge blocks of different topics/relationship
                                      types) but also ensures the relevance between the generated content and the context, avoiding knowledge
                                      fragmentation.
                                          In the KG-RAG architecture, the generation module is responsible for integrating the retrieved information
                                      and naturally generating answers or text content. Based on the current mainstream pre-training generation
                                      models, such as T5 or BART, the module has strong language modeling and text generation capabilities.
                                          The input of this module consists of two parts:
                                          Results of text retrieval channel: highly relevant document paragraphs from Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR)
                                      provide basic semantic support and background information for user queries.
                                          Semantic context of graph path: Multi-hop paths or triplet structures extracted from the knowledge graph
                                      are converted into readable text by natural language template conversion (such as "Entity A is superior to entity
                                      B and connected to entity C through relationship R").
                                          The generator will concatenate and fuse these two kinds of information and send them into T5 or BART
                                      model through a unified input format. In this process, the knowledge information such as entities, relations
                                      and events provided by KG path can guide the generator, and improve the knowledge, accuracy and logic of the
                                      generated content.
                                          In addition, to further optimize the generation effect, the system also introduces a content control mechanism
                                      to ensure that the generated content can revolve around the key information in the graph, while avoiding factual
                                      errors or semantic drift.
                                          In general, the generation module, based on the fusion of context information and with the help of powerful
                                      language generation ability, realizes high-quality answers and content output based on knowledge enhancement,
                                      reflecting the advantages of deep combination of knowledge graph and generation model.

                                  Key technical details
                                      In the KG-RAG framework, to improve retrieval and generation performance, the key lies in how to select
                                      the most valuable paths from a massive knowledge graph and effectively convert them into natural language
                                      prompts, thereby enhancing the expressive power of the generation model. This section introduces two core
                                      technologies: knowledge path selection mechanism and Knowledge-to-Text Prompting fusion strategy.

                                      Knowledge path selection mechanism
                                      In the face of complex knowledge graph, how to select the most relevant path from many entities and relationships
                                      is the key to model performance. KG-RAG adopts the following two strategies to improve the semantic accuracy
                                      of path selection:

                                          Graph Convolutional Network (GCN), a type of Graph Neural Network (GNN): Entities in the knowledge
                                      graph are represented as nodes in the graph, and relationships as edges. By using multi-layer graph convolutional
                                      networks, GCN obtains context-aware representations of entities. GCN can automatically aggregate information
                                      from neighboring nodes, thus capturing potential multi-hop semantic relationships. As shown in Fig. 8:

                                          Path Scoring Mechanism: Using the Path Attention Mechanism (Path Attention), weights are assigned to
                                      different paths. The path score not only considers structural distance but also combines the relevance of query
                                      semantics with target entities. For example, for the query "symptoms of COVID-19," the match with the path
                                      "COVID-19causesfever" is significantly higher than other secondary paths.

                                          Through such mechanisms, the model can select one or more most expressive knowledge paths before
                                      generation, providing key support for the generation of content.

                                          To improve the accuracy and relevance of knowledge path selection during the retrieval-augmented
                                      generation process, we design a path attention mechanism that dynamically evaluates and weights candidate
                                      knowledge graph paths based on their importance to the input query.

                                          First, each candidate path retrieved from the knowledge graph is encoded as a sequence of entity and relation
                                      embeddings. These sequences are then passed through a bidirectional GRU (BiGRU) to generate a fixed-length
                                      vector representation for each path, capturing both semantic content and structural information.

                                          Next, the input query is encoded using a pre-trained language encoder (e.g., BERT). We compare this query
                                      representation with each candidate path representation using a learnable attention scoring function. This scoring
                                      function assesses how semantically and structurally relevant each path is with respect to the query.

                                          After computing scores for all candidate paths, we apply a soft attention mechanism to assign normalized
                                      weights to each path. These weights reflect the relative importance of each path in supporting the final answer
                                      generation.

                                          Finally, the weighted sum of all path representations forms a composite knowledge vector, which is then
                                      passed into the generation module--either by direct concatenation with the query representation or through a
                                      prompt fusion layer. This enriched representation allows the model to ground its responses in the most relevant
                                      and trustworthy knowledge graph paths.

                                          This mechanism enhances the model's ability to perform multi-hop reasoning and filter out noisy or irrelevant
                                      knowledge, thereby improving overall generation quality and factual consistency.

                                      Knowledge-to-text prompting fusion strategy
                                      The transformation of structured triplet information into natural language prompts (Prompt) is the bridge
                                      connecting knowledge graph and language generation model. For example:

                                          [Entity: COVID-19] [Relation: causes] [Entity: fever].
                                          Translated into natural language: "COVID-19 may cause fever."
                                          This transformation is conducive to the better understanding and utilization of structured knowledge by the
                                      generation model. In practical applications, KG-RAG supports a variety of fusion methods:
                                          Concat Spliced fusion: The retrieved text and the transformed knowledge Prompt are directly spliced as input
                                      sequences for T5/BART processing, which is simple to operate and stable in effect.
                                          Attention-based fusion (Attention-based Fusion): In the input coding stage, cross attention mechanism
                                      is introduced to enable the generator to dynamically focus on key knowledge sentences, so as to highlight
                                      information.
                                          Memory mechanism (Memory-Augmented Fusion): Build an external knowledge memory unit that can be
                                      queried, and the generator can dynamically read the entity description and reasoning information in it during
                                      the generation process to improve the ability to deal with complex questions and answers.
                                          This knowledge path extraction and hint fusion mechanism provides KG-RAG with flexible and powerful
                                      knowledge utilization ability, making the generated results more logical, knowledge and context-related.

                                      Impact of fusion strategies

                                      ablation experiments with three KG-RAG variants:

                                      weighting before being input to the generator.
                                          Variant B: Multi-input Fusion Only.
                                          Semantic information from each channel is separately encoded and passed to the generator while retaining
                                      structural separation.
                                          Variant C: Combined Fusion Strategy (Soft+Multi-input Fusion, i.e., Our Method).
                                          This model integrates both mechanisms to leverage local attention-based fusion and structural diversity from

Evaluation indicators
For different types of tasks, quantitative and qualitative evaluation criteria are designed respectively:

(1) Question answering task indicators:

    Exact Match (EM): The percentage of answers generated that match the standard answer exactly.
    F1 Score: The overlap degree between generated answers and reference answers at the word level is measured
by the comprehensive precision and recall rate.

(2) Dialogue task indicators:

    BLEU-1/2/4: Evaluate the n-gram overlap between the generated response and the reference answer.
    ROUGE-L: Measures the similarity between the generated sentence and the longest common subsequence
of the true answer.
    Knowledge F1: Whether the key entities and relationships in the knowledge graph are correctly mobilized in
the generated reply.

(3) Manual assessment (optional):

    Knowledge Groundedness: Artificial judgment of whether the response depends on real knowledge.
    Relevance & Fluency: Judging the coherence and quality of the generated content.

Conclusion and future outlook

This study proposes an innovative Knowledge Graph Augmented Retrieval and Generation (KG-RAG) model,
which combines structured knowledge graphs with generative models to optimize knowledge retrieval and
generation for tasks such as open-domain question answering, medical question answering, and knowledge-
enhanced dialogue systems. Experimental results show that KG-RAG outperforms existing baseline methods
across multiple tasks, particularly after the introduction of knowledge graphs, where the model demonstrates
significant improvements in semantic understanding, reasoning ability, and generation quality.

(1) Open domain question answering task: By combining dual-channel retrieval (text retrieval and KG path
     retrieval), KG-RAG effectively improves the accuracy and completeness of answers, especially in WebQues-
     tions data set, where the model shows stronger entity alignment and cross-domain knowledge understand-
     ing ability.

(2) Medical Q&A Task: By leveraging the UMLS knowledge graph, KG-RAG can more accurately understand
     complex relationships in the medical field, enhancing its ability to reason and generate medical knowledge.
     Its performance on the PubMedQA dataset is particularly outstanding, significantly improving the model's
     coverage and accuracy of medical knowledge.

(3) Dialogue system task: By introducing the entity relationship in the knowledge graph, KG-RAG improves
     the context consistency and knowledge relevance of dialogue generation, making the generated dialogue
     more coherent and natural, especially showing stronger reasoning ability in multi-round dialogue.

    In summary, the KG-RAG model can effectively address the shortcomings of traditional generative models
by introducing knowledge graphs, especially excelling in knowledge-intensive tasks, demonstrating stronger
capabilities in knowledge reasoning and semantic expression. Our experiments show that KG-RAG not only
achieves significant performance improvements in traditional tasks but also provides efficient solutions for more
complex and domain-specific tasks.

    Although the KG-RAG model has achieved remarkable results in many tasks, there are still several aspects
that can be further optimized and expanded. Future research work can focus on the following directions:

(1) Fusion of multimodal knowledge graphs: Future research can explore how to integrate multimodal (such as
     visual and audio) knowledge graphs with KG-RAG models to enhance the model's performance in multi-
     modal tasks. For example, in medical image analysis and autonomous driving, combining visual knowledge
     graphs with text knowledge graphs will further improve the model's ability to understand complex environ-
     ments.

(2) Automatic Update and Expansion of Knowledge Graphs: Current knowledge graphs are static in many
     application scenarios, lacking the ability to update promptly and learn dynamically. Future research can
     focus on building adaptive mechanisms for updating knowledge graphs, enabling them to automatically
     expand and update according to actual application environments and needs, thereby better meeting the
     requirements of real-time question answering and dialogue systems.

(3) Construction of Cross-Language and Cross-Cultural Knowledge Graphs: In the context of globalization,
     cross-language and cross-cultural question-answering tasks will become increasingly important. Future
     work can explore how to construct multilingual and cross-cultural knowledge graphs, enhancing the

www.nature.com/scientificreports/

     cross-linguistic reasoning and generation capabilities of KG-RAG models in different language and cultural
     environments.
(4) Efficient Knowledge Path Selection and Fusion Strategies: Although we have proposed path selection meth-
     ods based on graph convolutional networks (GCN) and path scoring mechanisms, selecting and optimizing
     knowledge paths efficiently remains a challenge in complex tasks. Future work can further enhance the
     efficiency of path selection mechanisms and explore more efficient fusion strategies, such as path selection
     algorithms based on deep reinforcement learning, to optimize the scheduling and generation process of
     knowledge paths.
(5) Synergistic Evolution of Knowledge Graphs and Large-Scale Pre-Training Models: Currently, large-scale
     pre-trained language models (such as GPT and BERT) have achieved remarkable results in many tasks.
     In the future, we can explore the deep integration of knowledge graphs with these pre-training models,
     leveraging the powerful language generation capabilities of pre-training models and the strong structured
     knowledge advantages of knowledge graphs to further enhance the performance of models in complex
     reasoning tasks.

Through the above research and optimization, we hope to further promote the wide application of knowledge
graph-based generation model in various practical applications, especially in intelligent question answering and
dialogue systems in professional fields such as medical, legal and financial fields, to provide more accurate and
efficient solutions.

    In this study, we proposed the KG-RAG model, which effectively integrates knowledge graphs with retrieval-
augmented generation techniques to improve performance on knowledge-intensive tasks such as open-domain
question answering and dialogue systems. Although our model achieves promising results, there remains room
for improvement in reasoning efficiency and knowledge update capabilities.

    For future work, we plan to explore widely developed deep learning techniques, such as graph neural networks
(GNNs), self-supervised learning, and multimodal fusion, to further enhance the model's representation capacity
and reasoning depth. In particular, inspired by the approaches presented in [htt ps : / /do i .or g /1 0.1 0 17 /AT SI P. 20

complex multi-hop reasoning and domain-specific question answering tasks. Additionally, we will investigate
lightweight model designs and online knowledge updating mechanisms to improve real-time responsiveness
and adaptability to dynamic knowledge changes.

    In summary, by integrating these cutting-edge techniques, we expect the KG-RAG framework to achieve
greater breakthroughs in accuracy, efficiency, and applicability. It is worth noting that the optimization
of KG-RAG for retrieval relevance (domain-adapted DPR, path attention scoring) and diversity (explicit
constraints, dual-channel fusion) is the key to its surpassing of traditional RAG in generation accuracy and
comprehensiveness. The improvement in retrieval quality is directly translated into advantages in the factual
consistency and knowledge coverage of generated content, providing an effective paradigm for the collaborative
optimization of "retrieval-generation" in knowledge-intensive tasks.
